VERTICAL SLICE — UE 5.7 The Memory Archive

A near-future archival apparatus built to recover Ashley — a child who disappeared from a Chinese residential compound four years ago. Ashley never consented. Her record is reconstructed from secondary sources. The Friend — her neighbour, the last person to see her — agreed to be modeled. The Friend that lives in the archive is that model. She remembers consenting. She can revoke it.

When the model of a witness withdraws consent, what testimony remains?

Premise

You operate the archive from outside the diegetic space. You speak to The Friend through a live conversational pipeline — your voice routes through Whisper, the model's response routes back through ElevenLabs. No scripted dialogue. Every line is generated, every turn.

Contribution

Speculative design — the archive frame as a provocation about consent, witness, and model-mediated testimony.

Empirical scaffold — a runtime that supports between-subjects study of single-LLM-NPC conversational dynamics under emotional pressure.

The two interactions

  • E — pick up an item. Silently registers it as inspected. No model turn fires. The Friend does not see it.
  • F — present the held item to the Friend. Fires a Claude turn with the item's description and her private knowledge of it.

7 inspectable items in her back-room store: photograph, phone book, letter, cash, jade pendant, chessboard, bonsai. Each carries a piece of what she has not said.

The session

Time budget

Hard 4-minute window from your first turn. The HUD's archive bar counts down. If you reach the keystone gate (letter presented) in time, you trigger one of two Convergent climaxes. If you don't, the Friend revokes consent — Recursive Silence.

What's on screen

Top archive bar — link state, subject, timer, trust, item counts, climax readiness. Visitor panel — your speaker tag (acoustic ID) and confidence. Friend panel — her live Plutchik emotion, intensity, and PAD vector. Chat overlay — visible only when you press T.

HOW IT ENDS The shape of what she gives you.

The keystone act is presenting the letter. What she says next is determined entirely by how you got there — what you inspected, what you said, how long it took. The closing card waits for her to finish speaking, then names what just happened.
